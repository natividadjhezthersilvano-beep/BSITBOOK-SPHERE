Computer Networks and Internetworking

Computer networking is the connectivity among computers that allows communication between computer systems and devices. A computer network is a group of computers or devices connected to each other so they can exchange information and share resources.

Networks allow people to communicate, access information, transfer files, use shared printers, and connect to services through the Internet.

Learning Objectives

After studying this lesson, students should be able to:

  • Explain computer networking and network services.
  • Identify the different types of computer networks.
  • Recognize the cables and transmission media used in networking.
  • Explain internetworks, intranets, extranets, and the Internet.
  • Describe how the Internet works and identify its services.
  • Explain IP addresses and the Domain Name System.
  • Identify the advantages and disadvantages of the Internet.
  • Differentiate websites, webpages, web portals, and web browsers.
  • Recognize Internet service providers and Internet connections.
  • Explain how search engines are used to find information.

Network Services

Network services are functions and resources provided through a network. They allow connected users and devices to communicate, exchange information, and access shared resources.

Email Service

Email service allows users to send and receive electronic messages through a network. It can be used to exchange text, documents, pictures, and other attachments.

File Service

File service allows users to access, store, and share files through a computer or network storage system. It makes it possible for multiple users to access shared files.

Print Service

Print service allows users connected to a network to print documents using printers shared through that network.

Web Surfing

Web surfing allows users to open webpages, visit websites, and explore information available on the Internet.

Website Filtering

Network administrators can filter or block unwanted websites to control the websites that users can access.

Remote Network Access

Remote access allows authorized users to connect to a network from another location, such as their home or another workplace.

Virus Definition Updates

Networks can provide updates for antivirus software installed on connected workstations. These updates help computers identify newer threats.

Network Login

Network login services allow authorized users to sign in using their accounts and access permitted network resources.

Network Address Assignment

A computer needs a network address to communicate with other devices. Without an appropriate address, it may not be able to access the Internet or other network resources.

Types of Computer Networks

Computer networks can be classified according to the geographical area they cover and the way devices are connected.

Local Area Network (LAN)

A Local Area Network is a network that covers a small geographical area. It is commonly used in homes, offices, classrooms, and groups of buildings such as schools.

A LAN allows connected computers and devices to communicate and share resources within a limited area.

Examples of LAN use include:

  • Computers connected in a school computer laboratory.
  • Office computers sharing printers and files.
  • Devices connected to a home Wi-Fi router.

Wide Area Network (WAN)

A Wide Area Network covers a large geographical area. It connects LANs and other networks so that users and computers in different locations can communicate.

WANs can connect networks across cities, regions, countries, and even different parts of the world.

Examples include:

  • A company connecting offices in different cities.
  • A bank connecting its branches to a central network.
  • The Internet, which connects networks around the world.

Metropolitan Area Network (MAN)

A Metropolitan Area Network connects two or more Local Area Networks or Campus Area Networks within a town, city, or metropolitan area.

It covers a larger area than a LAN but generally does not extend beyond the boundaries of the city or metropolitan area. Routers, switches, and other network devices can be used to connect the networks.

Examples include:

  • Connecting several school campuses within one city.
  • Connecting government offices across a metropolitan area.
  • Connecting different branches of an organization within a city.

Wireless LAN and Wireless WAN

Wireless LAN (WLAN) and Wireless WAN (WWAN) are wireless equivalents of LAN and WAN. They use wireless communication technologies instead of relying entirely on physical cables.

Wireless networks commonly use electromagnetic waves, such as radio waves, to transmit information between devices.

Examples of Wireless Networks

  • WLAN: A Wi-Fi network used in a home, school, or office.
  • WWAN: A wireless network that provides connectivity over a wider geographical area.

Different Kinds of Network Cables

Networks use different types of transmission media to connect computers and other devices. These include twisted-pair copper wire, coaxial cable, optical fiber, and wireless technologies.

Devices can be connected over short distances, such as through Bluetooth, or across very long distances through interconnected networks.

Copper Wire Cable

Copper wire cables transmit information through electrical signals. They are commonly used in wired network connections.

Coaxial Cable

A coaxial cable is a type of electrical cable that contains a central copper conductor surrounded by insulating and protective layers.

It is used to carry electrical signals and has been used in television systems and some networking applications.

Parts of a Coaxial Cable

  • Outer plastic sheath: Protects the cable from external damage.
  • Copper screen: Helps protect the signal from interference.
  • Inner dielectric insulator: Separates the central conductor from the surrounding shield.
  • Copper core: Carries the electrical signal.

Fiber Optic Cable

Fiber optic cables transmit information using light signals through thin strands of glass or plastic. They are used for high-speed data transmission and communication over long distances.

Wireless Technologies

Wireless technologies allow devices to communicate without requiring a physical cable between them. They use signals transmitted through the air.

Examples include:

  • Wi-Fi
  • Bluetooth
  • Wireless mobile networks

Internetwork

An internetwork is formed when two or more networks or network segments are connected using networking devices such as routers. It allows communication between devices belonging to different networks.

Internetworks can connect public, private, commercial, industrial, and government networks. Modern internetworks commonly use the Internet Protocol (IP).

The module discusses these types of internetworks:

  • Intranet
  • Extranet
  • Internet

Intranet

An intranet is a private network that uses Internet Protocol and IP-based tools, such as web browsers. It is controlled by a single organization or administrative entity.

Access is generally limited to authorized members of the organization. It is commonly used by companies, schools, and other institutions to share internal information.

Examples of intranet resources include:

  • Internal announcements.
  • Employee or student portals.
  • Shared documents and files.
  • Internal communication systems.

Extranet

An extranet is a private network that allows limited access to selected users outside an organization. It can connect an organization with trusted external groups, such as business partners, suppliers, or customers.

Access is restricted and usually requires authorization.

Internet

The Internet is a worldwide interconnection of computer networks. It connects government, academic, public, and private networks, allowing users around the world to exchange information and access online services.

The module describes the Internet as developing from the Advanced Research Projects Agency Network (ARPANET), which was developed by the United States Department of Defense's research agency.

Intranet, Extranet, and Internet

Intranet

  • Private network within an organization.
  • Access is generally limited to authorized members.
  • Used for internal communication and resources.

Extranet

  • Private network with limited external access.
  • Connects an organization with selected outside users.
  • Used for collaboration with trusted partners.

Internet

  • Worldwide network of interconnected networks.
  • Connects users and devices around the world.
  • Provides access to many public online services.

World Wide Web (WWW)

The World Wide Web, commonly called the Web, is a system of interconnected documents and resources that users access through the Internet.

Using a web browser, users can view webpages containing text, images, videos, and other multimedia content. They can move between pages by clicking hyperlinks.

The World Wide Web was created in 1989 by Sir Tim Berners-Lee while working at CERN in Geneva, Switzerland.

Internet and World Wide Web

The Internet and the World Wide Web are related, but they are not the same thing.

  • Internet: The worldwide network infrastructure that connects computers and devices.
  • World Wide Web: A system of webpages and online resources accessed through the Internet.

How the Internet Works

The Internet connects computers and networks so that information can be exchanged. Connected computers may belong to government agencies, universities, companies, organizations, and individuals.

Client and Server

Many Internet services operate using the client/server model. A client is a computer or application that requests or receives a service. A server provides information, files, or other services to clients.

Client

A client is a device or application that requests information or services from a server. For example, a web browser acts as a client when it requests a webpage.

Server

A server is a computer or software system that provides information, files, or services to other devices.

Internet Service Provider (ISP)

An Internet Service Provider is an organization or business that provides users with access to the Internet and related services.

Users connect through an ISP to access websites, send email, transfer files, and use other Internet services.

Internet Services

The Internet provides different services that allow people to communicate, share information, transfer files, and access resources from different locations.

Electronic Mail (Email)

Email is a popular Internet service used to send and receive electronic messages. Messages can be transmitted quickly, and users can read and respond whenever convenient.

Email also allows users to attach files such as documents, forms, and pictures.

Common uses of email include:

  • Sending personal and professional messages.
  • Sharing documents and images.
  • Receiving school or workplace announcements.
  • Communicating with people in different locations.

Mailing List

An electronic mailing list allows a message or newsletter to be sent to multiple people at the same time.

It is useful for announcements, newsletters, group updates, and information sharing.

Newsgroups

Newsgroups are online discussion groups similar to electronic bulletin boards. They allow people to discuss specific topics and share questions, opinions, and information.

Some newsgroups are moderated. A moderator reviews messages before they are published. Discussions may also be organized into threads so that related messages are grouped together.

Chat

Chat is an Internet communication service that allows people to exchange messages in real time. Unlike traditional email, chat allows users to communicate through an ongoing conversation.

Examples include:

  • Online messaging applications.
  • Website live chat.
  • Group chat services.

File Transfer Protocol (FTP)

File Transfer Protocol is a standard method for transferring files between computers over a network. It can be used to upload files to another computer or download files from it.

Common uses include:

  • Uploading website files to a server.
  • Downloading files from a remote computer.
  • Transferring documents between systems.

Telnet

Telnet is a network protocol that allows a user to connect to and interact with a remote computer through a terminal. The remote computer is called the host.

After connecting, the user can enter commands on the remote computer. Telnet is an older remote-access method and does not encrypt its communication, so secure alternatives are preferred for modern systems.

Internet Address and IP Address

Computers and devices connected to a network use addresses to identify themselves and communicate with other devices. An Internet Protocol address, commonly called an IP address, identifies a device or network interface.

IPv4 Address

An IPv4 address consists of four numbers separated by periods. Each number represents an eight-bit section called an octet.

Example:

192.168.1.1

IP addresses allow data to be directed to the correct destination across networks.

Why IP Addresses Are Important

  • Identify devices on a network.
  • Allow devices to communicate.
  • Help route data to its destination.
  • Support access to Internet services and resources.

Domain Name System (DNS)

The Domain Name System helps users find resources on the Internet by translating human-readable domain names into IP addresses.

Remembering numerical IP addresses for every website can be difficult. DNS makes access easier by allowing users to enter familiar domain names instead.

For example:

www.example.com

When a user enters a domain name, the DNS system helps locate the corresponding IP address so the browser can connect to the correct server.

Domain Name Format

A domain name may contain a hostname, subdomain, and top-level domain.

Example:

www.example.com
  • www: A commonly used hostname or subdomain.
  • example: The registered domain name.
  • .com: The top-level domain.

Common Domain Name Extensions

  • .com: Commonly associated with commercial organizations.
  • .gov: Used by government institutions in supported domain systems.
  • .net: Originally associated with network-related organizations.
  • .org: Commonly associated with organizations.
  • .in: Country-code domain associated with India.

Country-code domain extensions identify a country or territory. For example, .in is associated with India.

Advantages of the Internet

The Internet provides many benefits in communication, education, entertainment, business, and access to information.

Communication

The Internet allows people to communicate with others around the world. Email, chat, and online communication services make it possible to exchange messages quickly.

Information

The Internet provides access to information about many subjects. Users can search for educational materials, news, research, tutorials, and other resources.

Entertainment

The Internet provides different forms of entertainment, including online games, music, videos, and other digital content.

Online Services

Many services are available through the Internet. Examples include online banking, job searching, ticket booking, and other online transactions.

E-Commerce

Electronic commerce, or e-commerce, refers to business transactions conducted through electronic networks. The Internet allows customers and businesses to buy and sell products and services online.

Examples include:

  • Shopping for books and household products online.
  • Ordering food through online platforms.
  • Buying and selling products through online stores.

Disadvantages of the Internet

Although the Internet provides many benefits, it also presents risks and problems that users should understand.

Theft of Personal Information

Personal information, such as names, addresses, passwords, and financial details, may be stolen or misused if users do not protect their accounts and information.

Spamming

Spamming refers to sending large amounts of unwanted electronic messages. Spam messages may contain advertisements, misleading information, or harmful links.

Virus and Malware Threats

Viruses and other malicious software can affect computer systems and interfere with their normal operation. Malware may damage files, steal information, or disrupt computer activities.

Inappropriate Content

The Internet contains content that may be inappropriate or harmful for certain users. Filtering tools and responsible browsing practices can help reduce exposure to unwanted content.

Safe Internet Practices

  • Use strong and unique passwords.
  • Avoid opening suspicious links and attachments.
  • Keep operating systems and applications updated.
  • Use trusted security software.
  • Do not share sensitive personal information with unknown sources.
  • Check whether websites and online services are trustworthy.

Website and Webpage

Webpage

A webpage is a document or individual page that can be viewed using a web browser. It may contain text, images, videos, links, and other visual or interactive elements.

Website

A website is a collection of related webpages that belong to a person, company, school, organization, or government agency. These pages are usually connected through navigation links.

Examples of websites include:

  • School websites.
  • Company websites.
  • Government websites.
  • News websites.
  • Online shopping websites.

Website vs. Webpage

Website

  • A collection of related webpages.
  • Usually contains several sections or pages.
  • Has a common domain name or web address.

Webpage

  • An individual document on the Web.
  • Contains specific information or content.
  • Can be part of a larger website.

Web Portal

A web portal provides a single point of access to different types of information, resources, and services. It may combine several tools and content areas in one location.

Examples of portal features include:

  • User login and account access.
  • Announcements and notifications.
  • Learning materials and online resources.
  • Schedules and other information.
  • Links to different services.

Web Browser

A web browser is a software application that allows users to access, display, and interact with webpages. It can display text, images, videos, music, and other information found on websites.

Examples of Web Browsers

The module lists the following browsers and their historical introduction dates:

  • Mosaic: April 22, 1993.
  • Netscape Navigator: October 13, 1994.
  • Internet Explorer: August 1995.
  • Opera: 1996.
  • Mozilla Firefox: 2004.

These are examples from the original learning material. They are not a list of the most popular browsers today.

Browser Main Menu

The browser menu provides access to commands and settings used when browsing websites. Depending on the browser, it may include options for opening pages, saving content, viewing history, and changing settings.

Toolbar

A toolbar contains buttons or controls that provide quick access to common browser functions.

Address Bar

The address bar is the area where users enter the address or URL of a website they want to visit. It can also display the address of the current webpage.

Internet Service Providers (ISPs)

An Internet Service Provider, also called an ISP or Internet access provider, is a business or organization that provides individuals and businesses with access to the Internet and related services.

Users generally subscribe to an Internet service to connect their devices or networks to the Internet.

Types of Internet Connections

Internet connections allow computers and other devices to access online services. The module groups connection types into home user connections and business connections.

Home User Connections

Dial-up Connection

Dial-up is a form of Internet access that uses a telephone line. A computer connects to an Internet Service Provider through a modem, which establishes a connection using telephone-line signals.

Modem

The word modem comes from modulator-demodulator. A modem converts digital information into signals suitable for transmission and converts received signals back into digital information.

Modems allow computers and other devices to communicate through transmission systems that carry signals.

Digital Subscriber Line (DSL)

DSL, or Digital Subscriber Line, is a technology that provides digital data transmission over the wires of a local telephone network.

DSL allows Internet data to be transmitted through telephone-line infrastructure.

Broadband Wireless Access

Broadband wireless access provides high-speed wireless Internet and data network access over a wider area.

Cable Modem

A cable modem provides Internet access through the infrastructure used by cable television systems. It allows broadband Internet data to travel through a cable network.

Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN)

ISDN is a communication technology that allows multiple types of communication, such as data, voice, video, and fax, to be transmitted through a digital telephone line.

The module explains that ISDN can support multiple simultaneous connections and can be used for communication such as videoconferencing, including voice, video, and text.

Business Connections

The module identifies DSL and SHDSL as business connection types.

DSL

DSL provides digital data transmission through telephone network wires. It can be used by businesses that need Internet connectivity through telephone-line infrastructure.

SHDSL

SHDSL stands for Symmetric High-speed Digital Subscriber Line. It is a type of DSL technology designed to provide symmetric data transmission, meaning upload and download speeds are designed to be similar.

Search Engines

The Internet contains a large amount of information, but finding a particular topic or resource can be difficult. Search engines help users locate information by searching through indexed webpages and displaying relevant results.

Users enter words or phrases related to the information they want to find. The search engine then displays a list of results that may contain the requested information.

Examples of Search Engines Listed in the Module

  • Google
  • Yahoo
  • MSN
  • Netscape
  • AltaVista Search

Some names in this list are historical examples from the provided learning material.

How to Use a Search Engine

A search engine can be used to find information by entering a keyword or phrase in its search box.

  • Open a search engine website.
  • Find the search box provided on the page.
  • Type a word, topic, or sentence describing the information you need.
  • Press Enter or select the search button.
  • Review the list of search results.
  • Open a result that appears relevant to your topic.
  • Return to the results and try another link if the information is not useful.
  • Change your keywords or try another search engine when necessary.

Search Keywords

Keywords are words or phrases entered into a search engine to describe the information a user wants to find.

For example, a user who wants information about roses may search for:

Types of roses

Search engines may also provide other services, such as email and other online tools.

Key Takeaways

  • Computer networking connects devices so they can communicate and share information.
  • Network services provide functions such as email, file sharing, printing, and remote access.
  • LAN, WAN, MAN, WLAN, and WWAN describe different types of networks.
  • Networking media include copper wire, coaxial cable, fiber optic cable, and wireless technologies.
  • An internetwork connects two or more networks.
  • An intranet is a private organizational network, while an extranet provides limited access to selected external users.
  • The Internet is a worldwide network of interconnected networks.
  • The World Wide Web is a system of webpages and resources accessed through the Internet.
  • Internet services include email, mailing lists, newsgroups, chat, FTP, and Telnet.
  • IP addresses identify devices and help route information across networks.
  • DNS translates domain names into IP addresses.
  • The Internet provides communication, information, entertainment, online services, and e-commerce.
  • Internet users should protect their personal information and avoid unsafe online activities.
  • A website is a collection of webpages, while a web portal provides access to multiple resources and services.
  • A web browser allows users to view and interact with webpages.
  • ISPs provide Internet access to individuals and organizations.
  • Internet connection types include dial-up, DSL, wireless broadband, cable modem, ISDN, and SHDSL.
  • Search engines help users locate information by entering keywords or phrases.
